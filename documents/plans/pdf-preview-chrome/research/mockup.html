LabShelf: PDF preview

The PDF pane beside your .tex or .typ source. The header acts, the footer reads. Hover a citation to see the paper and your highlights on the page you cited; underlined citations have a fix in their card. F8 jumps to the next problem in the PDF. Double-click the PDF to open the source line.

/ 3
  • Two thin bars. The header acts: Build on the left (Recompile and its options, intro.tex only, logs, Export), View on the right (Compare Versions, Mark version, dark PDF, pages, zoom). The footer reads like a status bar: where you are in the document on the left; build status, citations, limit and pre-flight on the right. Colour appears only where something needs attention.
  • Out of date. The PDF never dims. When the source changes, Recompile goes from muted to full colour with a small amber dot, and the footer says Source changed. Replacing a key or updating an entry from a citation card edits the source, so you see this state right away; auto compile runs on save.
  • Shortcuts to existing commands, not new features: Compare Versions (LabShelf: Compare Versions), Mark version (LabShelf: Mark Version, which asks for a name such as “sent to advisor”) and Pre-flight (LabShelf: Check Before Submitting, results in the Problems panel). Each tooltip names its command.
  • New in the preview: next problem (F8 / Shift F8, or the arrows by the citation count) walks undefined citations, broken references, overfull lines and entries to update; the undefined-key card suggests a key from your shelf with Replace; a citation with a page shows your highlights on that page first, the rest folded; the page and word limit; intro.tex only with \includeonly, shown only in projects that use \include; Export with an arXiv bundle whose .bib holds only cited entries.
  • Limit has no venue presets: set a page limit, a word limit and where counting stops. It counts the main text across every \include and \input file and is saved per root file.
  • The thesis text, entries and highlights are written for this mock-up.
